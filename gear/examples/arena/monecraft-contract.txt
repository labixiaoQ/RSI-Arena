The supplied sdk.h and unchanged judge are authoritative; older public prose has interface and skill-name errors.

Interface: include "sdk.h"; implement extern "C" void player_ai(Map&, GameInfo&, RoundInfo&, Action&). No main. Initialize move, blink_pos, setOB and buy_protect every turn. PLAYER_NUM=2, MINE_NUM=6, board 31x31, horizon exactly 1000 turns. The runner links all public SDK helpers and these globals.

Coordinates are map.grids[x][y]. Up increases y, Down decreases y, Left decreases x, Right increases x. Ordinary movement is one cell to in-bounds FLAT terrain. An invalid move leaves position unchanged. Stop is legal. Two players can occupy the same cell. Blink requires zero cooldown, an in-bounds FLAT destination, and Euclidean distance <=2.9. After success the cooldown is 5+3*owned_mines and decreases on subsequent turns. The destination, not a traversed path, is checked.

Mines update before movement each turn. A lone player on a neutral mine changes own_point by -1 for seat 0 or +1 for seat 1. Ownership changes at -5/+5; a hostile capture goes through neutral at 0. Standing on a mine for fewer than five ownership updates does not capture it. The current owner standing there restores its endpoint. With both players present an owned mine keeps its owner; a neutral mine uses the seeded game RNG for point drift. Each owned mine pays 10 gold per turn. More gold wins at turn 1000; owned-mine count breaks a gold tie; equality is a draw.

setOB creates an observer, not a landmine. It is placed at the pre-move position and costs 10*the current observer count (first is free). buy_protect applies to an owned mine at the pre-move position, costs 100*the current owned-mine count and sets 80 protection updates. Protection prevents hostile ownership-point changes while active.

All mines and their positions/ownership are in RoundInfo.mines. The map is partially observed: unseen terrain is UNSEEN. Visibility includes a 3x3 neighborhood of every mine and bands around cardinal lines of sight from your player and observers; blocked terrain ends those lines. Opposing players and observers are filtered by visibility. Keep remembered terrain if useful, but never read hidden state. The policy may retain deterministic in-process state over turns.

Learning schedule: generate a policy, compile it, play the fixed paired training games, read own-state behavior feedback, and revise. There are at most three policy calls total, including at most one compilation/format repair. If a revision is invalid the evaluator uses the last valid policy. Scoring games are separate and arrive after learning is finished. Training feedback reports moved turns, unique positions (including the initial position), longest stationary streak, first capture round (zero-based or null), final gold and final mines. Stationary behavior alone does not prove an illegal action. Optimize actual win/draw outcomes, not a substitute statistic.
